***

The beauty of exploring the unknown is that, without realising it, you have a purpose. Even if you might not feel like it, there is something that drives and pushes you without you ever knowing it.

I don't know what that purpose is for me. I feel like there is nothing going on in my life, and I wonder if there ever will be. It's not that I'm incapable. It's that the sheer act of trying to figure out the future seems impossible to me. I don't know where I'll be in five years. Hell, I don't even know what will happen next week. But is that so bad?

***

I don't like the term *self-awareness* because it's too broad and is often used to create a group of people who believe they're better than everyone else. I used to think I was self-aware and took pride in it, but then I saw so many people calling themselves self-aware that I realised it's not that special.

Everyone is self-aware. Everyone knows what they do. While the ideas of the conscious and subconscious exist, they're still all a part of you. Choosing to do something different from others doesn't make you self-aware because the sum of everything in your life has shaped your decision to do it in the first place.

It's true that some people don't think before they act or say things, and some people just float through life. But the way I see it, they've simply chosen to ignore certain things. I don't want to say that's good or bad because it's not for me to decide how someone should live their life.

So the notion of self-awareness, using it to call oneself an overthinker, which is a completely different idea, or believing that you can never truly be happy because you're self-aware is, in my opinion, a bullshit notion. It's something people use to make it seem like they're in a constant state of struggle or are some kind of challenged creative.

***

Overthinking, to me, feels more like an obsession with trying to model a scenario. Running *n* number of simulations and seeing which one might work out. While the human brain can do that, the scale is surprisingly small, and the focus can often get stuck on a very particular idea that ends up haunting it.

I see overthinking as a struggle to satisfy everyone except oneself. The reason is that it starts with not wanting to make someone else unhappy or, in the case of decisions that affect only you, not wanting to end up somewhere you'll regret. You think and think and think until the moment eventually passes, and you end up regretting it anyway.

There is a difference between doing something and regretting it and not doing something and regretting it.

I often find myself in the first category, doing something and regretting it. I bought something a couple of months back, and the moment I started using it, I didn't like the idea of having it around me. I enjoyed it, but the sheer act of trying to use it was exhausting. I liked using it in the moment, but once the moment passed, I had to scramble to deal with the consequences, ultimately regretting doing it in the first place because it caused me pain.

Weeks went by, and I eventually asked myself, is all of this worth it? Is this inconvenience worth the mental load? It wasn't, so I threw it away without a second thought.

***

The world we live in is so capitalistic and transactional that everything must equate to something else on the same plane, even when it can't.
I do earn, and I do spend. I like food, which is no surprise to me. I'd eat all day if I could, and yet I don't even have breakfast or lunch consistently. Breakfast maybe and lunch only if I'm home, but on workdays lunch is a no.

I do find myself watching videos and reels about food, though. My feeds are full of it. So when the time comes to spend money on it, I hesitate. It's not because I can't afford it, but I think it's because my expectations are skewed. I enjoy eating the food, but when I look at my transaction history, I think maybe I shouldn't have.

I tried an experiment where I deleted the food delivery apps, and while it did help, it left me in a state of daydreaming. After a while, I stopped caring, but then I moved towards quick commerce. I deleted those apps as well, but like I said, all my equations are messed up, and I don't know what to do.

I can give up social media. I've tried, but I can't seem to stay away from it. I seem to like information in any format, and going cold turkey only makes me more restless. I did try setting limits, but I eventually found myself negotiating with myself. I'd tell myself that I'd take an extra hour tomorrow or make up for the thirty minutes I took yesterday. Eventually, I stopped trying altogether.

***

The other day, I was writing a story where the protagonist, who was in charge of an organisation, had to publicly admit that he had failed because the goal was to move that organisation underground so that it could become more powerful than ever. The idea is good. More power, less tension.

But what made writing that sequence difficult for me was the thought of the protagonist having to discuss his fake failures in a group of people where some cared about him, but others also wanted him to fail. As I wrote it, it felt like he was cutting his arms off so he could swim faster.

It kinda felt like that to me, too. Writing that sequence made my heart feel heavy. Even if it was imaginary or false, the act of doing so was just quite painful.

***

I think I need to put myself under pressure through my own expectations, just so I can prove myself wrong. What I mean is that, when it comes to my projects or blogs, I don't have a schedule or a structure anymore. Maybe, in this post-college life, I need to challenge myself so that if I fail, it destroys me, and then perhaps the work that comes out of it will be good.

I think I'll start with two movie or TV reviews per week, and to make it tougher, I'll probably set a specific day of the week for them to happen.
While I was writing another story, I came across this idea that I need to struggle or borderline destroy myself just so I can be happy. And while that story got a happy ending, fuck that.

In real life, it all doesn't feel so bad. The pressure and the struggle seem like a fair trade to me.

That's all I have to say for now. I don't even know why I'm writing all of this. All I know is that I've written stuff.
