***

Well, well, well.

October flew past like a goddamn bullet. I have very little recollection of what exactly happened during most of it, but I'm still able to piece things together, so here goes.

***

Slate-wise, I never really set a slate, so here's a brief of what I've been up to. Mostly F1 race recaps and my end of college blog post.

Apart from that, I watched *Clayface* in theatres, and it was so much fun. More than the movie itself, this was the first movie I've watched by myself since, I believe, *John Wick: Chapter 4*. I've seen other movies in theatres, but those were with friends or family. Doing something by myself is something I haven't done in a while, and it was nice to just go out alone.

Review wise, I have my review of *Lanterns* pending. I generally dictate my thoughts and then use that material to build the review. It's almost like I'm pretending to give an interview or a speech, and that's something I've been doing for a while now. So I have all that content, and I'm in the process of framing and editing it. The show is nice, and I have a lot to say, but with *VisionQuest* airing, *Lanterns* genuinely feels like horseshit occasionally.

Like, with *VisionQuest*, I thought it would be a four- or six-episode show, with 30-minute episodes, and that it would all take place within Vision's mind. I imagined it would be a thought experiment, something philosophical or to be frank, fucking boring.
But my oh my, I was proven wrong in every single way.

As of today, I've watched six episodes, and the seventh was released today, so I'm yet to watch that one. But holy fuck, it's so good. It's giving us so much and isn't holding back even a little bit. And James Spader as Ultron is absolutely killing it.
I'll talk more about it after the season finale, but seeing this from a superhero show and learning that *VisionQuest* and *Lanterns* had the same budget really makes me wonder what the hell *Lanterns* was doing.

*Lanterns* is still good in my books, and I'm not going to fall into the trap of constantly thinking about what could have been. I'll ask the question, sure, but I'm not going to marry it. This conflict is making it difficult for me to write my *Lanterns* review from a place where I can focus entirely on the show itself.

I think I'll just postpone the review until after *VisionQuest*. I can probably release both around the same time, if I'm being honest.

***

I wrote a mini about a week ago, just some thoughts and stuff. I spoke about my idea of self-awareness and then later talked about what I was doing, why I hated certain things and what I needed to do.

I realised, while reviewing the entire thing, that I had contradicted myself slightly. Nothing major, but I essentially said that calling oneself self-aware is just a way of making oneself seem like they're in a constant state of struggle or are some kind of challenged creative.

The contradiction comes towards the end of the mini, where I said that I need to put myself under pressure and essentially pretend I'm struggling when I don't necessarily have to, or when I'm clearly not, just so that I can figure things out.

I get that it's a bit of a conflict because I'm saying self-awareness is a crutch, while the solution to my problems doesn't have a name but has the same characteristics I described. It's really put me in a conundrum because I don't want to be a hypocrite. And if I'm being honest, saying those things didn't really change anything. I forgot about them a day later, so I don't even know what I was going on about.

But it does make me sit down and ask myself a question: **What is success for me?**

***

## **How do I make myself believe I'm not a failure and everything is going to shit?**

I'll start by asking myself whether my life is truly static. Do I really not have literally anything going on? Am I just waking up, brushing my teeth, going to work, working, coming back, eating, sleeping and doing it all over again?
I think writing this mini itself says that isn't the case.

I'm realising that I measure how fulfilling my life is based entirely on whether something is completed, whether it succeeds or fails.

I'm essentially waiting for the result rather than letting the journey take me wherever it does. Which is funny because going where the wind flows and stuff like that is a philosophy I pride myself on having.
Let's take a proper look at my life. What am I doing outside the routine? Not going to judge, just taking stock.

## Papercraft

I started building a papercraft model of an F1 car, the Red Bull RB21 in its 2025 Japanese GP livery. I love that design so much that when I came across a Reddit post where someone had built one, I said to myself that I must have it.

So, on September 5th, I went and bought the paper needed for it, 140 GSM, because you need that extra sturdiness. I learnt about the different types of paper and what exactly GSM means so that was fun.

I started working on it and i love it. The precision required at that scale was something I had to slowly learn to get good at. I made a couple of changes to the PDF, removing some of the logos I didn't like and changing the rear wing to say Red Bull instead of the usual stuff because I liked how it looked.

I made those changes and started piecing things together. I kept building whenever I could, mostly on weekends if I had the time, so things slowed down around the beginning of October. However, I still had this unfinished car sitting around. I had completed the monocoque, the front nose and the rear section, so all that was left were the wings, wheels and a few other small components. It still looked pretty nice even in that state.

But last week, I decided I wanted to complete it.

I printed out the remaining parts, and on Monday night, November 2nd, I started putting together the major components. I ended up sleeping at 5 AM on Tuesday, but I had completed both the rear wing and the front wing. Those weren't too time-consuming, but the suspension was an entirely different story.

I had to roll almost 21 tiny pieces of paper and then fit them into these ridiculously small slits. It took quite a while, but I finished it, and that was another major component done. There was also quite a bit of cutting involved, which added to the time.

Later on Tuesday night, November 3rd I stayed up again to finish the other major components, mostly the tyres. I have them assembled now, and one thing I actually went rogue with was painting parts of the car. The floor was mostly black, but I didn't like the greyish appearance it had because of the printing. I'd printed it in true black, but it was still bothering me, so I painted it, and it looks nice. I went even more rogue and painted the tyres black as well. I do plan to add some design to them, so that's still a work in progress.

I did use tape and stuff, and I honestly don't care if someone says you're not supposed to do this. **My paper, my car, my time.**
Anyway, all that's left now is attaching these components to the car, finishing the smaller aero bits and printing a new halo because I misaligned the old one.

Today, the 4th, I think I'll print the new halo and fix that. I should be able to finish the smaller aero bits across the car and pretty much assemble everything except the wheels. I'll paint those, keep them ready and attach them tomorrow.

#### Audio

I have these knockoff AirPods Max, and I've been looking for headphones but I'm not paying that much for a pair, even if an angel is going to sing in my ears. One of the arm broke, so I repaired it, and now they sit properly on my head again.

One thing I've noticed, though, is that I can genuinely hear the differences between wireless earbuds, wireless headphones, wired earphones and the pair of wired studio headphones I have, even when others might not
They all have their strengths and weaknesses.

The wired headphones are a bit bulky and can sometimes get in the way at my desk. I can't necessarily go running with them either.

My wireless earbuds are good, but they keep falling out of my ears, and the available sizes don't fit particularly well. When I'm running or even sitting, I end up constantly adjusting them, which completely takes away my focus.

The wireless headphones are nice. I can walk around wearing them, and even when I'm sitting at my desk watching a movie, I get a decent experience. I can also lean back and relax a little more. Prolonged use does hurt after a while, but it's not too much of a biggie.

#### Pokémon

Another fun thing I did was collect these Pokémon themed delivery bags from Blinkit, the quick-commerce app. I'd heard about them before but never actually came across one, so I didn't know how much I would like them. When I finally got my hands on one, I loved it.

I first got Jolteon and Vaporeon, and I ended up painting them. Later, I got Blastoise and Onix and that was even more fun because I learnt different painting techniques.

I really like how they look. I've set them up on this wall bookshelf I have, and they're quite nice to look at. I also learnt quite a bit about colour theory and stuff along the way, so that was a fun little experience.

#### Beyond Money

My money app, Beyond Money, which I use to track my personal finances, finally received an update to its public version. It's been almost six months since the last update, so this was long overdue. The reason it finally happened was that I was a bit bored and decided to completely overhaul the design of the app.

It looks very nice now, and I might even do another overhaul next year. I also upgraded some features and functionalities, added an API for live data and made a few other improvements. I'm looking towards adding the SMS scraper but that will take a bit of time and research regarding systems.

I updated the homepage as well, so it's much more inviting and informative now.

***

Generally, I've also been working on two other applications. One is a content scraper for Medium, while the other is a stats dashboard that's much more refined and detailed than what the platform itself offers.

My backend upgrade of my tech ecosystem. I've finally finished ideating, and I'm now looking for time to start the final phase, which is implementation.

I also have an app-lock application that I'm testing for Android.

I recently got Canva Pro Lite for "free" as part of my mobile plan, which is good because, for my race recaps and general artwork, whenever I needed premium features, I used to rely on my club's Canva account. I can still use that account, of course, but having my own subscription just makes things easier.

I did consider looking at it from the perspective that I'm getting this much later than I would have liked, but I think that's a bad way of looking at it because i still have plenty of use for it.

I think that's about it. I can't recall anything else I've done, and apart from all these things, I do other stuff as well, whether it's cleaning something, fixing something or rewatching *Ocean's 8* for the hundredth time.

Many of these things are ongoing, and that feeling of completion and the satisfaction that comes with it haven't quite hit me yet. I guess that's why I often feel like I have nothing to do.

I won't suddenly become all grateful. I'll start complaining very soon, but I think that deciding to complete the papercraft model made me realise that I do need to force myself to be a little more proactive in my life.

Let's see where November takes us. The penultimate month of the year, and with everything that's happened so far, there's a hell of a lot more to do.

**Time to tear my life a new asshole.**
